Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Mountain View

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. From indoors, mark where the water enters, and let professionals handle everything else.
  5. Photograph the DamageTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Holes and fractures left in the roof after a branch or hailstones hit it.
  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.
  • Rubber boots around plumbing vent pipes that split after years in the sun.
  • Popped or exposed roofing nails, each one a tiny opening for rain.
  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Cavities

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. Often the one clue is paint that blisters or trim that swells at the floor.

Hidden Attic Mold

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. It may take hold in 24 to 48 hours and work its way over the underside of the sheathing.

Lights and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Map Where Water Went

Moisture meters and a thermal camera let us follow the rain through the attic, the ceilings and the walls, so spots that look dry from underneath are not missed.

2

Tarp and Protect

An exposed roof gets covered with a temporary tarp, or we line things up with your roofing contractor. Anything sitting beneath the damage, including the flooring, gets protected too.

3

Tear Out and Dry

We pull out insulation and drywall that cannot be saved, then run air movers and dehumidifiers in the attic and the rooms below, checking moisture readings until the framing is dry.

4

Sanitize and Repair

Where it is called for, we apply an antimicrobial to the bare framing. After the roofer has finished, new insulation goes in, fresh drywall is hung and finished, and the ceiling is primed and painted to match.
